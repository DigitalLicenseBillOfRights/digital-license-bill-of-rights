# Article I — Right to License Ownership

## Section 1. Purpose

This Article recognizes a consumer's qualifying permanent software license entitlement as a distinct statutory property interest.

## Section 2. Qualifying permanent purchase

A qualifying permanent purchase is a transaction in which a consumer pays consideration for a license represented, expressly or by its commercial substance, as permanent or perpetual rather than limited to a subscription term, rental period, trial, or other defined duration.

A qualifying purchase includes a permanent license acquired individually or as an identifiable component of a bundle. The commercial substance of the transaction controls; a publisher or distributor may not avoid this Act merely by assigning a contrary label in an end-user license agreement or other standard-form term.

A subscription, rental, trial, or genuinely time-limited access arrangement is not a qualifying permanent purchase when its limited duration and material conditions were clearly and conspicuously disclosed before purchase. A free or promotional entitlement is not automatically a qualifying purchase unless the publisher or distributor grants permanent rights or other law provides them.

No entity may disguise a permanent purchase as temporary access through unilateral terminology, hidden conditions, or post-purchase reclassification.

## Section 3. Statutory property interest

Upon a qualifying permanent purchase, the consumer automatically acquires a distinct statutory property interest in the purchased license entitlement, together with the rights established by this Act.

The interest belongs to the consumer and is not owned by a storefront, payment processor, publisher, authentication provider, or other intermediary merely because that entity administers the transaction or records the entitlement.

The entitlement may not be arbitrarily revoked, deactivated, or materially restricted except as permitted by this Act or other applicable law.

## Section 4. Intellectual property retained by publisher

Nothing in this Act transfers ownership of copyright, source code, trademarks, patents, trade secrets, or other intellectual property from the publisher or other lawful rights holder.

The consumer's statutory property interest concerns the purchased license entitlement and the rights necessary to exercise it. It does not confer a general right to reproduce or distribute software to the public, create additional licenses, or exploit the publisher's intellectual property beyond the permissions and limitations established by this Act.

## Section 5. Continuity

The license entitlement survives a storefront's or publisher's closure, dissolution, acquisition, restructuring, insolvency, or discontinuation of an authentication or distribution service, subject to the continuity and preservation provisions of this Act.

A business event or the failure of an intermediary does not, by itself, extinguish a permanent purchase.

## Section 6. Inheritance and Succession

1. A qualifying permanent license entitlement recognized under this Act constitutes inheritable personal property and shall pass upon the consumer's death in accordance with the consumer's valid will, applicable estate law, or applicable laws of intestate succession.
2. A publisher, storefront, distributor, or other covered entity may not terminate, revoke, deactivate, or materially restrict a qualifying permanent license solely because its owner has died.
3. An executor, administrator, personal representative, or other legally authorized representative of the deceased consumer's estate shall have the authority to preserve, administer, and transfer the license entitlement in accordance with applicable estate law and this Act.
4. An heir or lawful beneficiary receiving a license through inheritance shall receive the same license entitlement and associated statutory rights held by the deceased consumer, subject to the original license's platform designation, compatibility limitations, and applicable restrictions.
5. No publisher, storefront, or intermediary may require an heir or estate to repurchase the license or pay an inheritance, succession, activation, or transfer fee as a condition of exercising inherited rights.
6. Inheritance shall not create additional licenses or authorize simultaneous use beyond the concurrency rights associated with the original entitlement.
7. The rights established by this Section are non-waivable and may not be diminished by an end-user license agreement, terms of service, account policy, or other contractual provision.

## Section 7. Uniform baseline

The statutory baseline applies without arbitrary distinction based on purchase price, edition, publisher, storefront, distribution method, or whether the license was obtained individually or as an identifiable component of a bundle.

## Section 8. Effective application

The rights in this Article attach on the Act's effective date to all qualifying permanent licenses within the Act's scope, including qualifying purchases made before that date. A business implementation period may not postpone the consumer's acquisition or exercise of those rights.
