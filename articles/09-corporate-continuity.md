# Article IX — Corporate Continuity and Ownership Preservation

## Section 1. Continuity of consumer rights

A qualifying permanent license entitlement and the consumer rights attached to it survive a publisher's or storefront's insolvency, bankruptcy, dissolution, acquisition, restructuring, discontinuation, or closure.

No such business event automatically expires a consumer's permanent purchase.

## Section 2. Responsible entity

The publisher remains ultimately responsible for compliance with this Act even when it outsources distribution, authentication, account administration, hosting, or other essential services.

A covered entity may not avoid its obligations by relying on the failure, insolvency, or discontinuation of a contractor, platform, service provider, or intermediary.

## Section 3. Continued exercise

Covered entities shall take reasonable measures to preserve the consumer's ability to install, reinstall, authenticate, activate, and use qualifying software after a covered business event or service discontinuation.

## Section 4. Continuity planning

A publisher shall maintain a reasonable continuity plan for qualifying permanent licenses. As appropriate to the software and its technical architecture, the plan shall preserve or provide access to:
- Installation packages and executables;
- Required authentication or activation alternatives;
- License keys or entitlement-verification materials;
- Necessary dependencies and configuration materials; and
- Technical documentation reasonably necessary to continue use.

## Section 5. Preservation escrow and release

A publisher shall make reasonable provision for preservation of the materials necessary to maintain consumer entitlements, including through an independent custodian or successor arrangement where appropriate.

Release or activation of preservation materials shall occur upon objectively defined triggers, including insolvency, dissolution, permanent discontinuation of essential services, or other circumstances in which the publisher can no longer reliably provide the rights required by this Act.

## Section 6. Successors and transfers of business

Obligations under this Act attach to successors and entities that acquire or assume the relevant software, service, or business operations. A transfer, merger, acquisition, or restructuring may not be used to extinguish existing consumer entitlements.

## Section 7. Offline and local alternatives

Where technically capable, a publisher shall provide reasonable offline or local alternatives when a mandatory online service becomes unavailable or is permanently discontinued. This does not require recreating inherently cloud-based or remote functions that cannot reasonably operate independently.

## Section 8. No perpetual support obligation

This Act does not require a publisher to develop new features, provide indefinite technical support, or continue active development forever. It does require preservation of the consumer's purchased entitlement and reasonable means to continue exercising it as provided by this Act.

## Section 9. Implementation

Consumer rights attach immediately. Technical infrastructure, escrow arrangements, and continuity plans may be phased in according to statutory deadlines, but interim preservation measures shall be used where feasible. An implementation period may not authorize revocation, forced repurchase, waiver, or indefinite delay.
