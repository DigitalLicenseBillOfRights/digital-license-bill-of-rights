# Article VIII — Lending, Sale, Gift, and Transfer

## Section 1. Transferable entitlement

A qualifying permanent license is a transferable asset. A consumer may lend, sell, gift, or otherwise permanently transfer the license entitlement as provided in this Article.

## Section 3. Inheritance as a Distinct Form of Succession

1. Inheritance of a qualifying permanent license is a transfer by operation of law and is distinct from a sale, gift, loan, or voluntary transfer by the consumer.
2. A license passing through inheritance shall not be treated as a commercial resale, voluntary surrender, or abandonment of the entitlement.
3. A legally authorized estate representative may take reasonable steps to identify, preserve, document, and transfer the deceased consumer's qualifying licenses to lawful heirs or beneficiaries.
4. Covered entities shall provide reasonable procedures for verifying the authority of an estate representative and recording inherited ownership.
5. No marketplace fee, resale commission, or voluntary transfer fee may be imposed solely because a license passes through inheritance.
6. Inherited licenses remain subject to the original entitlement's applicable concurrency limits and other lawful restrictions. Inheritance does not create additional licenses.

## Section 4. Permanent transfer

A permanent transfer conveys the entire applicable license entitlement and the statutory rights associated with it. Upon completion, the transferor relinquishes the entitlement and may not continue using the software under that transferred license.

The recipient receives the original platform designation, compatibility scope, installation and use rights, and preservation rights. The recipient may not be charged a new activation or transfer fee merely because the entitlement changed hands.

## Section 4. Temporary lending

A consumer may temporarily lend a qualifying license while retaining ownership. The owner may reclaim the license at the end of the lending period. Simultaneous use remains subject to the number of licenses and the concurrency limits of the purchased entitlement.

## Section 5. Gifts

A consumer may gift a license without compensation. No publisher, storefront, or intermediary may impose a transfer fee for a gift or other uncompensated transfer.

## Section 6. Private and independent sales

Consumers may conduct private sales and use independent third-party services to sell or transfer qualifying licenses. No mandatory publisher or storefront approval, marketplace listing, or fee may be imposed as a condition of a lawful private or independent transfer.

## Section 7. Optional storefront marketplace

A storefront may offer an optional secondary marketplace. The combined fees imposed by that storefront for a transaction on its own marketplace may not exceed five percent (5%) of the final agreed resale price.

The cap applies to all fees imposed directly or indirectly by the storefront for that marketplace transaction, including stacked, renamed, or separately itemized charges. Fees must be disclosed before the transaction. A storefront may not require use of its marketplace as the exclusive means of transfer.

The five-percent cap is a cap on marketplace fees only. It does not limit or determine the consumer's resale price.

## Section 8. Unrestricted resale price

The seller and buyer may independently determine, propose, negotiate, and accept any resale price the market will bear. This Act establishes no statutory ceiling, floor, formula, or mandatory relationship between resale price and original purchase price, bundle discount, edition, age, or manufacturer's suggested retail price.

Nothing in this section requires a seller to accept an offer or prevents a buyer from declining it.

## Section 9. Fees not permitted

A publisher, storefront, or intermediary may not charge a transfer fee for:
- Gifts or other uncompensated transfers;
- Temporary lending;
- Private sales outside its optional marketplace; or
- Sales or transfers conducted through independent third-party services.

Taxes required by law may be collected if separately identified and accurately calculated. A genuinely optional service independently requested by the consumer is not a transfer fee, provided it is not required to complete the transaction or exercise statutory rights. No entity may disguise a transfer fee as a tax, service charge, processing charge, or other label.

## Section 11. Bundles and dependent content

A bundle's actual functional structure determines how its components transfer.

- A title and downloadable content (DLC) that is functionally dependent on that title shall transfer together as a package.
- Independent titles within a bundle may be separated, retained, or transferred individually.
- A mixed bundle shall be treated as containing individually transferable independent titles and any associated dependent-content package.
- Material dependencies must be disclosed before purchase.
- A publisher may not retroactively redefine independent content as dependent, or create artificial dependencies or bundles primarily to prevent transfer.

The functional relationship of the components, not merely the publisher's label, controls.

**The way software is bundled should determine how its components transfer, not whether the consumer has the right to transfer them.**

## Section 11. Implementation

The rights attach on the Act's effective date. Covered entities may phase in automated transfer systems but shall provide reasonable interim methods for verifying ownership and completing transfers where feasible. No implementation period may suspend the right to transfer.
