# Article XI — Enforcement, Legal Scope, and Non-waiver

## Section 1. Covered entities and jurisdiction

This Act applies to entities that sell, distribute, license, administer, authenticate, host, or otherwise control qualifying software licenses or essential services for consumers in the United States. Covered entities include relevant subsidiaries, affiliates, contractors, intermediaries, service providers, successors, and future operators to the extent of their role in the covered transaction or service.

The Act applies to foreign entities to the extent permitted by the jurisdiction of the United States and other applicable law.

## Section 2. Non-waiver

The rights established by this Act are nonwaivable. An end-user license agreement, terms of service, click-through agreement, account policy, arbitration clause, or other standard-form term may not waive, disclaim, or diminish a right protected by this Act. A conflicting term is unenforceable to the extent of the conflict.

## Section 3. Anti-circumvention

A covered entity may not structure a transaction, technical system, business arrangement, or contractual term primarily to evade or defeat this Act. Substance controls over labels and formal arrangements.

## Section 4. Minimum protections

The Act establishes minimum consumer protections, not maximums. Nothing in this Act shall be construed to reduce a stronger right or remedy available under federal law, state law, contract, or technical capability, except to the extent a specific and express provision of federal law requires otherwise.

Federal and state enforcement provisions shall be coordinated so that the Act does not unnecessarily displace stronger state consumer protections.

## Section 5. No retaliation

A covered entity may not retaliate against a consumer for exercising or attempting to exercise a right under this Act. Prohibited retaliation includes unjustified account suspension, license loss, ordinary service denial, discriminatory pricing, or reduction of unrelated functionality.

## Section 6. Judicial enforcement and remedies

A consumer aggrieved by a violation shall have an appropriate private right of action in a court of competent jurisdiction. Available remedies should include, as appropriate:
- Actual damages;
- Declaratory relief;
- Injunctive relief;
- Reasonable attorney's fees and costs; and
- Other relief authorized by law.

Congress and legislative counsel should specify penalties for repeated or willful violations, limitation periods, evidentiary rules, and procedures.

## Section 7. Arbitration and class actions

The legislative proposal is intended to preserve meaningful access to courts. It should provide that a pre-dispute agreement may not require arbitration of a claim under this Act or waive participation in a class action concerning such a claim.

Any arbitration of a dispute under this Act should be permitted only after the dispute arises, through a knowing and voluntary agreement separate from any standard-form end-user license agreement or terms of service, without penalty for declining. Such an agreement binds only consenting parties and does not remove a court's authority over class certification for nonconsenting parties.

Legislative counsel must draft an express and technically precise exception to the Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. § 1 et seq., if needed to accomplish this policy. This draft does not assert that the current language is a complete amendment to that Act.

## Section 8. Copyright coordination

Congressional counsel shall review and specify any amendments, exceptions, or cross-references necessary to coordinate this Act with 17 U.S.C. §§ 106, 117, and 1201, including rights relating to reproduction, archival copies, transfer, circumvention, and preservation.

This Article identifies issues for legislative drafting and does not claim that all copyright interactions have been resolved.

## Section 9. Public enforcement

The Federal Trade Commission and state attorneys general should be authorized, within their respective jurisdictions, to investigate and enforce violations. Congress should define coordination, notice, remedies, and any limits on duplicative recovery.

## Section 10. Retroactive application

The Act applies, from its effective date forward, to qualifying permanent licenses purchased before or after that date through a covered storefront, publisher, or service operating on or after the effective date.

Nothing in this Act requires the recreation of an entity or service that ceased to exist before the effective date. Obligations apply to covered entities, successors, and operators within the Act's scope on or after that date.

## Section 11. Immediate rights; phased business compliance

Consumer rights attach on the Act's effective date. Congress may establish phased deadlines for technical, operational, and administrative compliance, including a mandatory final compliance-by date.

During any implementation period, covered entities shall provide reasonable interim procedures or manual alternatives where feasible. No grace period may:
- Suspend or delay the consumer's underlying rights;
- Permit revocation or deactivation of a qualifying permanent license;
- Require repurchase as a substitute for compliance;
- Permit waiver or contractual diminution of statutory rights;
- Authorize obstruction or retaliation; or
- Become an indefinite delay or permanent exemption.

## Section 12. Effective date and compliance schedule

The Act shall take effect on the date specified by Congress. The sponsoring legislator and legislative counsel must establish a definite final compliance-by date and any phased milestones. No date is selected in this working draft.

## Section 13. Rule of construction

Any ambiguity concerning an implementation period shall be construed to permit additional time only for the means of compliance, not for postponement, suspension, or diminution of the consumer rights established by this Act.
