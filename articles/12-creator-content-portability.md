# Article XII — Creator Content Ownership and Portability

## Section 1. Scope and Creator Ownership

1. This Article applies to covered subscription services that provide consumers with tools to create, edit, process, or manage original content, including but not limited to text, documents, artwork, images, audio, music, video, designs, and other creative works.
2. Content created by a consumer through a covered creative service remains the intellectual property of the creator to the extent recognized under applicable law.
3. Payment for, use of, or participation in a creative subscription service does not, by itself, transfer ownership of creator-generated content to the provider.
4. A provider may not claim ownership of creator-generated content solely because it was created, stored, edited, or processed using the provider's software or services.
5. Nothing in this Article creates intellectual property rights in material that is not otherwise protected under applicable law.

## Section 2. Continuous Right to Export and Retain

1. Every consumer using a covered creative subscription service shall have a non-waivable right to export and retain local copies of their creator-generated content.
2. The ability to export creator-generated content shall be continuously available throughout the active subscription period. Providers may not restrict export availability to cancellation, expiration, account termination, or other limited circumstances.
3. Providers shall offer a straightforward, reasonably accessible export process that does not require specialized technical knowledge.
4. Exported content shall be available in:
   - A commonly usable final format appropriate to the content; and
   - An editable project format, including reasonably necessary project structure and components, where technically feasible.
5. Providers shall support multiple commonly used export formats when technically feasible and appropriate to the type of content.
6. Export processes shall preserve, where technically feasible and legally permissible, relevant layers, metadata, project structure, embedded creator-owned assets, and other components reasonably necessary for continued use or editing.
7. A provider shall not impose additional storage requirements, upgrade requirements, subscription renewal, or avoidable fees as a condition of exporting creator-generated content maintained within the consumer's applicable service storage allowance.
8. Providers may not deliberately degrade, corrupt, omit, or restrict exported content for the purpose of preventing its use outside the subscription service.

## Section 3. Protection of Provider and Third-Party Intellectual Property

1. Nothing in this Article transfers ownership of a provider's software, source code, proprietary tools, trade secrets, or legitimately proprietary assets to a consumer.
2. Providers may protect their own intellectual property and the legitimate intellectual property rights of third parties.
3. A provider may not claim exclusive ownership or proprietary status over assets that are in the public domain or are freely available under terms that permit their continued use and export.
4. The fact that an asset is available at no cost does not, by itself, establish that it is free of copyright or other licensing restrictions. Applicable rights and license conditions remain in effect.
5. Nothing in this Article expands a creator's rights to third-party assets beyond the rights granted by applicable law or the relevant license.
6. Export requirements shall not compel providers to disclose source code, proprietary software, or third-party materials that the provider has no legal right to redistribute.
7. Where a third-party or provider-owned asset cannot lawfully be included in an export, the provider shall, where technically feasible, identify the excluded asset and disclose the applicable limitation so the creator can understand what is necessary to continue using the project.
8. Providers shall not use legitimate intellectual property restrictions as a pretext to prevent consumers from exporting or retaining their own creator-generated content.

## Section 4. Cancellation and Post-Subscription Export Period

1. Cancellation or expiration of a creative subscription shall not extinguish the creator's ownership of their content.
2. Upon the effective cancellation or expiration of a subscription, providers shall preserve the consumer's ability to access and export creator-generated content for a minimum of seven (7) full calendar days beginning on the calendar day following the date the consumer's paid access ends.
3. The seven-full-calendar-day period is a statutory minimum. Providers may establish longer export periods but may not establish shorter periods.
4. The post-subscription export period shall begin on the calendar day following the date the consumer's paid access to the creative service ends. If cancellation is requested before the end of a paid subscription period, the export period begins on the calendar day following expiration of that paid access.
5. Providers shall give clear notice of the applicable export period, available export methods, and the date on which export access will end.
6. Export during this period shall not require subscription renewal, purchase of another product, or payment of an avoidable access or retrieval fee.
7. Providers are not required by this Article to maintain subscription tools, storage, or export infrastructure indefinitely after the minimum post-subscription export period expires.
8. Nothing in this Section permits a provider to delete, destroy, or claim ownership of creator-generated content during the applicable export period in violation of this Article or other applicable law.

## Section 5. Non-Waiver and Anti-Circumvention

1. The rights established by this Article are non-waivable.
2. No subscription agreement, end-user license agreement, terms of service, click-through agreement, account policy, or other contractual provision may eliminate or materially diminish these rights.
3. Providers may not condition export on renewal of a subscription, purchase of another product, or payment of an avoidable access or retrieval fee.
4. Providers may not structure their services, file formats, export procedures, or cancellation practices to circumvent or defeat the protections of this Article.
5. A provider may not retaliate against a consumer for exercising or attempting to exercise a right established by this Article.

## Section 6. Relationship to Other Rights

1. This Article establishes minimum consumer protections and does not diminish stronger rights provided under other applicable law.
2. Nothing in this Article requires a provider to continue offering creative software or subscription services after discontinuation.
3. Nothing in this Article requires perpetual storage or perpetual export access following the minimum post-subscription export period.
4. The rights established here are distinct from the permanent software license ownership rights established under Article I. A consumer's subscription to creative software does not automatically create permanent ownership of the software itself.
5. Nothing in this Article transfers ownership of provider software, proprietary tools, or third-party intellectual property to the creator.

## Section 7. Implementation

Providers shall implement reasonable export procedures, format support, and cancellation notices consistent with this Article. Technical implementation deadlines may be phased, but shall not suspend, waive, or diminish the consumer rights established herein.

## Section 8. Inheritance and Estate Access to Creator-Generated Content

1. Creator-generated content remains the creator's property, to the extent recognized under applicable law, and may pass to the creator's heirs, beneficiaries, or estate in accordance with applicable estate law.
2. A covered provider may not claim ownership of, delete, or deny reasonable access to creator-generated content solely because the creator has died.
3. An executor, administrator, personal representative, or other legally authorized representative of the creator's estate shall have a reasonable opportunity to access, preserve, and export the creator-generated content for estate administration and transfer to lawful heirs or beneficiaries.
4. Providers shall establish reasonable procedures for verifying the authority of an estate representative and facilitating access and export without requiring the representative to assume the deceased creator's personal identity or credentials.
5. Following verification of the estate representative's authority, the provider shall preserve access to the creator-generated content and provide a reasonable opportunity to export it for a minimum of ninety (90) full calendar days. This period shall begin on the calendar day following the date the provider verifies the representative's authority. Providers may allow a longer period.
6. Estate access and export shall not require subscription renewal, repurchase of the service, or an avoidable inheritance, retrieval, or access fee.
7. This Section does not require a provider to transfer ownership of its software, proprietary tools, or third-party intellectual property, or to provide access to content the creator did not own or have lawful rights to transfer.
8. Nothing in this Section requires perpetual storage or perpetual service operation. However, a provider shall provide the minimum estate access and export period established here and shall not use the creator's death as a basis for immediate deletion or forfeiture.
