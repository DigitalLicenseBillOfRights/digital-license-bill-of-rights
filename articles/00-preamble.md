# Preamble

## A proposed Digital License Bill of Rights

Digital software has become essential to modern life. Consumers rely on software for communication, education, work, entertainment, creativity, commerce, and access to information. Consumers routinely pay for software with the reasonable expectation that a permanent purchase will provide lasting ownership of the purchased license entitlement.

Digital distribution has separated payment from practical control. A consumer may pay in full yet remain dependent on a storefront account, publisher authentication server, license database, or other third-party service. A business closure, platform change, account decision, or discontinued server can make a purchased product inaccessible even when the consumer has paid for a permanent license.

This Act establishes a distinct statutory property interest in a qualifying purchased software license entitlement and protects the consumer's ability to install, reinstall, use, preserve, share, lend, transfer, sell, and continue accessing that entitlement under the conditions set out herein.

The Act does not transfer copyright, source code, trademarks, patents, or other intellectual property ownership from a publisher. It distinguishes ownership of the consumer's purchased license entitlement from ownership of the intellectual property embodied in the software.

The rights established are minimum protections, not maximum limits. Nothing in this Act should reduce a stronger right available under other law, contract, or technical capability.

Consumer rights attach on the Act's effective date. Phased implementation may be provided to covered businesses solely to complete necessary technical, operational, and administrative changes. Such implementation periods do not suspend consumer rights.

**You bought it. You own the license. Your rights should follow you.**
