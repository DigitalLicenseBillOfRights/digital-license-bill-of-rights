# Article V — Physical Media Parity and Successor Generations

## Section 1. Scope

This Article applies to consumer software on consoles, handheld systems, personal computers, tablets, mobile devices, smart televisions, consumer electronics, and successor hardware platforms.

## Section 2. Physical media parity

A consumer who purchases a qualifying permanent digital license shall receive no fewer fundamental installation, reinstallation, use, preservation, and transfer rights than a consumer who lawfully acquires equivalent software on physical media, subject to genuine technical compatibility and the other provisions of this Act.

Digital delivery alone may not be used to impose a more restrictive ownership model.

## Section 3. Independent installation

Where technically capable, a consumer shall be able to install and reinstall a qualifying license without being perpetually dependent on the original storefront or distribution service. This includes reasonable migration to compatible replacement hardware.

## Section 4. Protected successor generations

The minimum protection covers the original platform and the next two true successor hardware generations, where the software is technically capable of operating on those generations. This is a minimum, not a maximum; additional generations remain protected where technically feasible.

A consumer may not be required to repurchase the same platform-family software solely because a compatible successor generation has been released.

## Section 5. Meaning of a generation

A successor generation is determined by the totality of objective technical changes, including material changes to architecture, core capabilities, and software functionality. Marketing labels alone do not control.

The following do not, standing alone, create a new generation:
- Mid-generation performance upgrades;
- Pro, Elite, Slim, or similar variants;
- Processor-speed or graphics improvements within substantially the same architecture;
- Storage or memory changes;
- Minor changes to dimensions, cooling, power efficiency, or industrial design.

A hardware refresh is not a new generation.

## Section 6. Genuine incompatibility

A publisher may establish that a particular version cannot operate on a successor device because of a genuine technical limitation. The limitation must be objectively supportable and may not be manufactured or asserted solely to force repurchase.

## Section 7. No artificial repurchase

A covered entity may not use a mid-generation refresh, compatibility label, storefront change, or minor hardware revision to require a new purchase where the software remains technically compatible within the protected platform family.

## Section 8. Implementation

The rights in this Article attach on the Act's effective date. Technical implementation may be phased, but reasonable interim methods shall be provided where feasible. Nothing in an implementation period suspends the consumer's underlying entitlement.
