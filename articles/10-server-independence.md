# Article X — Server Independence and Community Continuity

## Section 1. Purpose

Consumers who purchase qualifying permanent licenses should not lose the practical ability to use software solely because an essential online service is discontinued or materially unavailable. This Article establishes preservation and transition duties while recognizing that some functions inherently depend on remote infrastructure.

## Section 2. Preservation materials

Upon permanent discontinuation of an essential online service, the responsible publisher shall provide or make available, as reasonably necessary for continued lawful use:
- Dedicated server software or executable server components;
- Configuration, deployment, and administration tools;
- Relevant protocols, interfaces, and API documentation;
- Matchmaking, discovery, and direct-connection tools;
- Alternative authentication or entitlement-verification methods;
- Required documentation and dependencies; and
- Other technical materials reasonably necessary to enable community-operated or independent alternatives.

The publisher need not disclose source code if binaries, tools, interfaces, and documentation are sufficient to preserve the relevant functionality.

## Section 3. Final compatibility update

Where feasible, before a planned shutdown the publisher shall provide a final compatibility update that removes unnecessary dependence on the discontinued service and enables reasonable alternatives, such as community servers, local play, direct connection, offline operation, or alternative authentication.

## Section 4. Notice

For a planned shutdown, the publisher shall provide at least 90 days' notice to affected consumers. Emergency, security, legal, or force majeure circumstances may justify a shorter period, but do not eliminate applicable preservation obligations.

## Section 5. Material unavailability trigger

An essential online service is materially unavailable when a consumer cannot use an essential feature required for ordinary operation or a core function of the software. This may include authentication, account verification, matchmaking, multiplayer, progression, or another server-dependent function that is essential to the product's ordinary use.

A service shall be treated as materially unavailable for purposes of this Article when either:
1. The service has been materially unavailable for 30 consecutive calendar days; or
2. The service has been materially unavailable for 90 cumulative days within a rolling 365-day period.

A publisher's status label, public statement, or characterization of the service does not override its actual availability to consumers.

## Section 6. Effect of trigger

When either threshold in Section 5 is met, the service is treated as discontinued for purposes of the preservation and continuity obligations in this Article and Article IX.

The publisher shall either restore reliable service or provide the preservation materials and resources necessary for reasonable community or independent alternatives. The threshold does not automatically shut down official servers and does not, by itself, require release of source code. A publisher may continue repairing or operating its official service, but must concurrently meet the preservation obligations triggered by this Article.

## Section 7. Restoration and interruption accounting

A temporary, partial, or intermittent restoration does not reset the 30-day consecutive period unless the service is reliably restored and remains reliably available. A brief restoration shall not erase days counted toward the 90-day cumulative threshold.

Once the 90-day cumulative threshold has been reached, a later restoration does not extinguish preservation obligations already triggered. Routine maintenance and brief interruptions that do not materially prevent ordinary use are excluded.

## Section 8. Offline and local functionality

Where technically capable, software shall support offline or local operation when essential online services are unavailable or discontinued. This may require reasonable modification even when the software was originally designed as always-online.

Nothing in this section requires recreating functionality that inherently depends on remote computation, live third-party data, or infrastructure that cannot reasonably be replicated. Security and legal requirements remain applicable and may not be used as pretexts to defeat preservation rights.

## Section 9. Implementation

Consumer rights and preservation triggers attach immediately on the Act's effective date. Technical materials and transition systems may be phased according to statutory deadlines, but an implementation period may not postpone a preservation trigger or permit avoidable destruction of materials needed to comply.
