# Digital License Bill of Rights

**A proposed United States federal framework for consumer ownership of permanently purchased digital software licenses.**

> You bought it. You own the license. Your rights should follow you.

## Status

This repository contains a **public-comment working draft**. It is a policy and legislative drafting proposal, not enacted law, legal advice, or a claim that the provisions have been formally vetted by legislative counsel. The draft is intended to invite scrutiny and improvement from consumers, software developers, publishers, storefronts, preservationists, technologists, attorneys, and policymakers.

The proposal's central rule is **minimums, not maximums**: statutory rights are a floor and do not displace stronger rights available under other law, contract, or technical capability.

## Core distinction

The proposal creates a distinct statutory property interest in the consumer's qualifying purchased license entitlement. It does **not** transfer copyright, source code, trademarks, patents, or other intellectual property ownership from the publisher. Storefronts and distribution platforms administer transactions and entitlements; they do not own the consumer's purchased license entitlement.

## Key design choices

- Consumer rights attach immediately on the Act's effective date, including qualifying permanent licenses purchased before that date.
- Businesses may receive phased time to implement technical and administrative systems, but that grace period does not suspend consumer rights.
- Qualifying permanent purchases are not converted into subscriptions merely by EULA labels.
- Consumers may set and negotiate any resale price. A 5% cap applies only to combined fees charged by a storefront operating its own optional resale marketplace.
- A genuine platform-generation change is distinguished from a mid-generation hardware refresh.
- The draft addresses preservation and community continuity when essential online services are materially unavailable or permanently discontinued.
- Qualifying permanent software license entitlements are inheritable personal property, subject to the original entitlement's platform, compatibility, and concurrency limits; inheritance is distinct from resale or voluntary transfer.
- Creator-generated content remains the creator's property to the extent recognized under applicable law and may pass through an estate. Verified estate representatives receive at least 90 full calendar days to access and export that content.
- Creative subscription services must provide continuous, non-waivable export rights for creator content, including usable final and editable formats and multiple formats where technically feasible, plus at least seven full calendar days after paid access ends to export content. The seven-day minimum for ordinary subscription expiration is distinct from the 90-day estate-access minimum.
- Providers retain their legitimate intellectual property, and the proposal does not impose unlimited storage obligations.
