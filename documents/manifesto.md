# Digital Ownership Manifesto

## Rights, not permissions. Ownership, not temporary access.

A purchase should mean something.

When a consumer pays for a permanent software license, that purchase should create a lasting entitlement—not a revocable promise dependent entirely on a company's continued operation.

Digital delivery should not mean diminished ownership.

A storefront may deliver the software. A publisher may own the copyright. An authentication provider may verify an entitlement. But none of those roles should erase the consumer's purchased license.

We believe:

- A permanent purchase should remain permanent.
- A consumer's license entitlement should be recognized as a distinct property interest.
- Publishers should retain their intellectual property.
- Storefronts should administer purchases, not own consumers' entitlements.
- Consumers should be able to install, reinstall, preserve, and use what they lawfully purchased.
- A storefront change should not force a second purchase of the same platform version.
- Compatible successor hardware should not be an excuse for artificial repurchase.
- Families should be able to share within fair license-based limits.
- A permanent license should be lendable, sellable, giftable, and transferable.
- Consumers—not a statutory formula—should determine the price at which they sell their own license.
- A publisher's closure should not erase ownership.
- When essential servers disappear, preservation and community continuity should be possible where technically feasible.
- Statutory protections should be a floor, not a ceiling.

We recognize that not every online function can be recreated offline. We do not demand perpetual development or the release of every source code repository. We demand reasonable preservation, continuity, and alternatives so that a permanent purchase is not silently converted into a temporary one.

We also recognize that implementation takes work. Businesses may need time to build systems. But a technical transition cannot be used to suspend the rights that the law recognizes.

**Corporate failure does not erase consumer ownership.**

**Software should not have an expiration date determined solely by the business decisions of its publisher.**

**When a publisher closes its servers, the community should have the tools to keep the software alive.**

**Ownership without the right to transfer is incomplete ownership.**

You bought it. You own the license. Your rights should follow you.
