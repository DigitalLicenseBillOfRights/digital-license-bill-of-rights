# Settled Policy Decisions

This document records the principal policy choices reflected in the current public-comment draft. It is intended to prevent accidental drift while still allowing reasoned public challenge.

## 1. Minimums, not maximums

The Act establishes a statutory floor. It must not reduce stronger rights already available under law, contract, or technical capability.

## 2. Consumer rights attach immediately; business implementation may be phased

Consumer rights attach on the Act's effective date. A grace period may apply only to technical, operational, and administrative implementation. It does not suspend rights, authorize revocation or forced repurchase, permit waivers, or allow obstruction or indefinite delay. Reasonable interim procedures should be available where feasible.

## 3. Retroactive application to qualifying purchases

The framework applies to qualifying permanent licenses purchased before or after the effective date through covered storefronts or services operating on or after that date. It does not require resurrecting entities or services that ceased before the Act takes effect.

## 4. Ownership of the license entitlement is distinct from intellectual property

The consumer receives a statutory property interest in the purchased license entitlement and the associated rights. The publisher retains copyright, source code, trademarks, patents, and other intellectual property.

## 5. Resale price is unrestricted

A consumer and buyer may independently set, propose, negotiate, and accept any resale price the market will bear. There is no statutory price ceiling, floor, or formula tied to original price, bundle discount, edition, age, or MSRP.

## 6. The 5% cap is limited to optional storefront marketplace fees

The 5% cap applies to combined fees a storefront imposes for its own optional secondary marketplace. It does not cap the consumer's resale price. No fee is permitted for gifts, uncompensated transfers, lending, or independent private/third-party sales merely because the license changes hands.

## 7. Platform portability and cross-platform entitlement are distinct

The draft protects storefront portability on the same compatible hardware platform and successor-generation continuity. It does not automatically require a publisher to provide a separately developed version for a genuinely different hardware platform unless promised or later granted.

## 8. Successor-generation minimum

The baseline protects the original platform and the next two true successor generations where technically capable. Mid-generation refreshes and cosmetic or incremental hardware variants do not independently create a new generation. More generations remain protected when technically feasible.

## 9. Preservation is for personal lawful use

Personal backup and preservation rights do not authorize public distribution, creation of additional licenses, or use by extra people outside the Act's sharing rules. On permanent transfer, the transferor relinquishes the entitlement and may not retain or use a functional copy.

## 10. Server continuity has objective triggers

Essential online service material unavailability is measured by either 30 consecutive calendar days or 90 cumulative days in a rolling 365-day period. The threshold triggers preservation and continuity obligations; it does not automatically shut down official servers or require source-code disclosure.

## 11. Creator content ownership and portability

- Creator-generated content remains the creator's intellectual property to the extent recognized under applicable law. A creative subscription does not, by itself, transfer ownership of that content to the provider.
- Export is continuously available throughout an active subscription; it is not limited to cancellation or account termination.
- Export is non-waivable and must be straightforward and reasonably accessible.
- Export should include a commonly usable final format and an editable project format where technically feasible. Multiple commonly used formats are required where technically feasible and appropriate.
- Relevant project structure, layers, metadata, and creator-owned components should be preserved where technically feasible and legally permissible.
- Providers retain their legitimate rights in software, proprietary tools, trade secrets, and proprietary assets. Public-domain or freely available assets may not be claimed as the provider's exclusive property when the applicable terms permit continued use and export. No-cost availability alone does not eliminate copyright or license restrictions.
- Providers must identify and explain excluded provider-owned or third-party assets where technically feasible when those assets cannot lawfully be included in an export.
- Creators receive at least seven full calendar days to export content after paid subscription access ends. The period begins on the calendar day following the end of paid access. Longer periods are permitted.
- No subscription renewal, product purchase, avoidable retrieval fee, or additional storage upgrade may be required to export content maintained within the consumer's applicable service storage allowance.
- No unlimited storage or perpetual post-subscription export obligation is created. Existing applicable service storage limits remain in place.

## 12. Reuse license remains undecided

No LICENSE file is included. A public repository is not itself a grant of permission to reuse project text. The repository owner will decide separately whether and how to license the draft.

## 13. Mandatory compliance-by date remains open

A definite final compliance-by date and phased deadlines must be established by the sponsoring legislator and legislative counsel. No date has been invented in this draft.
