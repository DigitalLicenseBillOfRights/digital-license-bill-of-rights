# Contributing

Thank you for helping improve the Digital License Bill of Rights. This repository is open for public comment on a proposed federal legislative framework.

## Ways to participate

- **GitHub Issues:** Ask questions, identify unintended consequences, cite relevant law or technical evidence, and propose changes.
- **Pull requests:** Suggest specific text changes with a clear explanation of the problem the change addresses.
- **Review:** Comment on clarity, enforceability, accessibility, technical feasibility, consumer impact, and effects on publishers or platforms.

## Helpful issue format

Please include:
1. The article or passage involved.
2. The problem, ambiguity, or real-world scenario.
3. Supporting sources, examples, or technical evidence where available.
4. Suggested language or a practical alternative, if you have one.
5. Any trade-offs or unintended consequences you can identify.

Please keep one main proposal per issue where practical. Distinguish a proposed policy change from a drafting clarification.

## Settled policy versus open drafting

The [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) file records policy choices that have been deliberately settled in the current working draft. They may still be challenged with reasons and evidence, but please identify when a proposal would reopen one of those choices.

The [TODO.md](TODO.md) file lists questions that remain open for legislative counsel or further public review. In particular, a mandatory final compliance-by date has intentionally not been selected.

## Review expectations

- Be respectful and address arguments rather than people.
- Cite primary sources where possible.
- Do not post personal information, confidential information, or credentials.
- A pull request is a proposal, not a promise of acceptance.
- Maintainers may edit, defer, or decline proposals to preserve coherence and legal/technical review.

## Scope

The project concerns a proposed United States federal framework. Comments about state law, international law, copyright, consumer protection, software preservation, accessibility, security, and platform engineering are welcome when their relevance is explained.
